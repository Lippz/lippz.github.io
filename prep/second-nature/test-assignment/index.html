1 · Problem and framing

Walk me through your solution.

The problem was that managers could not easily tell who needed help. The average score excluded people who had not practised, and managers had to combine completion, attempts and scores in an export.

I designed the screen around the question: who should I follow up with, and what should I do? It opens on Need support and groups people by the kind of follow-up they may need: starting practice, continuing practice, or coaching.

2 · Problem and framing

Why are there no charts?

The manager quoted in the brief explicitly said they did not need more charts. They needed to know where to take action. A chart could show a distribution, but the follow-up is with a specific person.

I kept the average score as secondary information and made its denominator visible. I would consider a trend chart if research showed a separate need, such as reporting progress to leadership.

3 · Problem and framing

Is this really a dashboard?

I see it as an action-oriented dashboard. The summary shows the state of the team, and the list brings completion, attempts and scores together so the manager can decide what to do.

In the mockup, 21 people need support, 5 are just assigned and 16 are on track. The average score is 74, based on 28 of 42 people with attempts. These are example figures, not research results.

Most of the page is a working list rather than charts. That responds to the brief's request for a dashboard and the manager's need to identify actions. One limitation is that the design does not show change over time. A period-over-period change on a summary card could be a future addition.

4 · Problem and framing

Why these three groups?

The groups represent different situations and different kinds of follow-up. Someone who has not started may need a reminder to begin. Someone who has started but has limited practice may need a reminder to continue. Someone practising below the pass score may need coaching.

There are three groups but two actions: Remind and Coach. I removed a separate Nudge action because its mechanism was the same as a reminder; only the message and destination would differ.

Claude proposed the Below pass score group, and I retained it as a hypothesis. The grouping and thresholds still need validation.

5 · Problem and framing

What exactly counts as not enough practice?

That threshold is an assumption, not a defined rule in the mockup. The example rows show 25–50% completion, one or two attempts and more than ten days without activity, but those examples do not establish a validated threshold.

Before implementation, I would define the rule for each course with the product team and check it against real data and manager feedback.

6 · Layout and interaction

Why are the summary cards filters instead of tabs?

The cards and tabs repeated the same counts. I combined summary and navigation to remove that duplication and reduce a level of navigation. The three segments also add up to the team total: 21 plus 5 plus 16 equals 42 in this example.

The risk is that a card may look like static information rather than a filter. Selected and hover states help communicate the interaction, but I would test whether managers actually discover it.

7 · Layout and interaction

Where did the average score go?

The average was misleading when shown without context, so I made it secondary rather than removing it completely. It is still available on All reps, with the explanation that it is based on 28 of 42 people with attempts.

That makes it clear that the score does not describe everyone on the team, including those who have not started.

8 · Layout and interaction

A rep has three courses. Where do they appear?

The proposed rule is to show each person once, in their highest-priority support group, with the relevant course in the row. Claude proposed that rule, and I retained it because the manager acts on a person and the summary counts people, not course rows. The priority rule still needs to be defined precisely.

My addition was to open details for all of that person's courses when the manager clicks their name or row. A +2 more indicator signals additional courses with issues. The indicator is shown in the mockup, but the detail popup itself has not been designed.

9 · Layout and interaction

Why is there Remind all but no Coach all?

A reminder can use a shared message, while coaching requires an individual conversation. Claude proposed that distinction, and I kept it because the two actions have different needs.

The exact Coach interaction has not been designed in this mockup. I would clarify whether it should help schedule a conversation, prepare a coaching agenda, or connect to an existing workflow.

10 · Layout and interaction

Walk me through the bulk reminder.

Remind all opens a dialog showing the recipients, the recipient count and a prefilled message that the manager can edit. There is also an option, enabled by default, to skip people who received a reminder in the last seven days.

After sending, the design shows a confirmation toast, changes the affected row buttons to Reminded, and suggests checking back in three days.

The support count does not decrease just because a message was sent. A reminder is an action, not evidence that the person's situation has improved. The counts and confirmation should reflect the recipients actually included after exclusions.

11 · Layout and interaction

Why a confirmation dialog rather than send-with-undo?

The manager is sending a message to several people, and we cannot assume delivery can be undone. The dialog lets them check recipients and edit the message before sending.

For a single reminder, that dialog might be unnecessary friction. I would decide that after clarifying the messaging channel and what can genuinely be reversed.

12 · Layout and interaction

How does this work for 8 reps? For 500?

For a small team, all rows can be visible and empty groups can be omitted. Bulk actions are less important.

For a large team, the manager needs search, team filters and bulk actions. A long Show more list would not scale well. I would open the group in a filtered view with pagination, but that large-team view is not designed in the current mockup.

13 · Layout and interaction

What if nobody needs support?

That empty state has not been designed. I would show a clear message such as Everyone is on track, rather than leave an unexplained blank area.

The manager could still review newly assigned people or upcoming deadlines. I would keep those separate from support flags so we do not manufacture a problem just to fill the page.

14 · Edge cases

Why Just assigned, and why seven days?

Someone assigned a course yesterday may not have started, but that does not automatically mean they need intervention. Just assigned prevents those people from generating premature alerts.

Seven days is a placeholder, not a validated rule. I would prefer to relate the grace period to the course deadline and expected practice schedule, where those are available.

15 · Edge cases

What other edge cases did you consider?

The design considers people who have already received reminders and people with issues across several courses.

There are still gaps: someone on leave, a course without a pass score, someone who completed a course long ago and stopped practising, and a new manager with no data. I would clarify the rules for those cases before treating the grouping as production-ready.

16 · Edge cases

A rep is struggling in several courses. What happens?

I raised this edge case, and Claude proposed much of the detailed behaviour. The person still appears once, with an indicator for additional courses with issues. Opening their details would show the relevant courses together.

Several issues might indicate workload, unclear priorities or another shared barrier, but the screen cannot diagnose the cause. I would give the manager context for a conversation rather than assume a motivation problem.

A possible next step is a Plan 1:1 action in the detail view, with the courses forming the agenda. A bulk reminder should be consolidated into one message per person rather than one message per course. Those details are proposed behaviour; the popup has not been designed.

17 · Edge cases

You reminded a rep twice and nothing changed. What does the manager see?

The current mockup only shows that a reminder was sent and suggests checking back in three days. Repeated reminders and escalation are not designed yet.

A possible extension, suggested by Claude, is to show reminder history and recommend a conversation when reminders do not lead to practice. Two reminders is only an example threshold. I would validate when to escalate rather than automatically assume a third reminder is useless.

18 · Measurement

How would you measure success, and why these metrics?

I would look at time from opening the page to the first relevant action, whether people who had not started make a first attempt after a reminder, and whether managers still need to export the data.

I would also track reminders per person per week and flags that managers identify as incorrect. Those are guardrails against excessive messaging and unreliable recommendations.

A seven-day outcome window is an initial assumption. The under-one-minute target was suggested by Claude and is not based on a baseline. I would establish current performance before setting a target. These are proposed metrics, not results from this exercise.

19 · Measurement

Reminders go up, but practice does not. What now?

More reminders alone would not mean success. I would check whether they reached the right people, whether the message and destination were useful, and whether people had time or another barrier to practising.

I would also revisit the targeting rules and the screen itself, rather than assume the problem must be outside the design. That is why I separate the manager's action from the learner's outcome. To attribute improvement to reminders, we would need a suitable comparison, not just activity after a message.

20 · Process and AI

How did you use AI? Which decisions were yours?

Claude extracted styles from the public trial, assembled tokens, components and frames, and drafted copy and notes. I used it to support execution, and I also accepted some of its suggestions for the interaction logic.

My decisions included focusing on people and actions rather than more charts, using an actionable table, adding Just assigned, reusing a pattern from my App Radar work, and making bulk reminders a supporting interaction.

I also replaced tabs with filter cards, combined Nudge and Remind into one action, proposed opening all courses from a person's row, and raised the multiple-course edge case.

Claude proposed details such as the Below pass score group, showing a person once in the highest-priority group, and omitting Coach all. I retained those choices, but I would not present every detail as an independently developed or validated decision.

21 · Process and AI

You said the pattern was familiar from the start. How do you know it fits this problem?

I had solved a similar problem in App Radar, so I started with that pattern as a hypothesis. I checked it against the research in the brief, rather than assume it would transfer unchanged.

The summary cards and action-based groups carried over. But I needed to explain the average's denominator, account for newly assigned people, and handle people with several courses. I also replaced tabs with filter cards and combined Nudge and Remind.

I have not tested the pattern with Second Nature managers. The brief supports the direction, but usability and the grouping rules still need validation.

22 · Process and AI

The brief said about 60 minutes and low fidelity. Why is this so polished?

AI helped assemble the visual foundation from the product's existing styles, so visual fidelity required less manual effort. I used that foundation while working on the problem framing and interaction decisions.

That does not mean the exercise was fully validated or that I explored every state. I should also state the actual time I spent, rather than imply I met the suggested timebox if I did not.

23 · Process and AI

What are you least sure about? What would you do next?

The main uncertainties are the grouping thresholds, whether managers recognise the cards as filters, and how well the single-row approach works when someone has several courses with issues.

I would also clarify the Coach action and reminder channel, and design the empty and large-team states.

My next step would be to test the main workflow with a small group of managers, for example three to five, and check the grouping rules against real data. That would be a next step, not something already done.

24 · Process and AI

Engineering says this is too much. What do you cut?

I would keep the grouped list, a clear action per row and the distinction for newly assigned people. Those support the core task and help avoid premature alerts.

I would first simplify message customisation and, if necessary, make the summary cards non-interactive with a simpler way to filter. I would preserve a clear recipient check before bulk sending.

The exact scope would depend on which parts engineering identifies as expensive. I would discuss those costs rather than assume that a particular visual element is the main implementation problem.
